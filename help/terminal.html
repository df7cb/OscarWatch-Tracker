Tools → OscarWatch Terminal is a keyboard-to-keyboard packet window for working stations through satellite digipeaters, in the style of GreenCube Terminal. It talks to a packet soundmodem (Direwolf or the UZ7HO soundmodem) over AGWPE, shows every frame heard, tracks your contacts and logs them for you.

What you need

  • A radio with its audio connected to the PC, and PTT handled by the soundmodem (VOX, CAT or a serial line).
  • A soundmodem with its AGWPE server switched on:
    • UZ7HO soundmodem: open Settings → Devices, tick the AGW server and leave the port on 8000. Use it for IO-117, which needs the BPSK AX.25 1200 modem.
    • Direwolf: add AGWPORT 8000 to direwolf.conf (it is on by default in most builds) and use MODEM 1200 for the ISS.
  • Your callsign and grid in Settings → Station.

OscarWatch keeps tuning the radio for Doppler as usual. Terminal only sends and receives the packets, so keep CAT tracking running for the satellite you are working.

Getting started

  1. Start the soundmodem and check it decodes packets.
  2. Focus the ISS or IO-117 in the main window and open Tools → OscarWatch Terminal.
  3. Terminal connects to 127.0.0.1:8000 on its own. The status bar shows the modem state. If your modem is on another PC or port, change it in Settings → Modem; Test and list ports checks the connection and fills in the radio port list.
  4. When the satellite rises, frames appear in the message log. Press F1 to call CQ.

Message formats

Each satellite has a message format and digipeater path. Terminal picks them from the focused satellite:

SatelliteFormatPathModem
ISS (25544)APRSARISS1200 AFSK
IO-117 GreenCube (53106)Plain textnoneBPSK 1200 (UZ7HO only)

APRS sends a CQ as an APRS status (>CQ IO85) and a message to a station as an APRS message addressed to that call. Plain text puts the text straight in the frame with the To call as the destination, which is how GreenCube operators work. Other satellites with a packet or APRS digipeater in the satellite database get APRS with no path. Use the Format buttons in the window, or Settings → Satellites, to change a satellite's format, path or the name used for UHM.

The window

  • Shortcuts: F1 to F8 send your macros. F12 logs the station in the To box now.
  • Send message: type a call in To (or CQ) and a message, then press Enter or Send. TX delay waits a few seconds before sending, which helps on a busy pass. Messages are limited to 67 characters.
  • Station and statistics: one panel beside the shortcuts. Your call and grid, the satellite, then elevation, azimuth, range and LOS (or AOS while it is below the horizon) in one row, with an elevation bar under that row. Underneath are the pass counts: unique callsigns, frames you sent and received, how many of your frames came back through the digipeater, the median round-trip delay, and how many UHM users are active. Reset clears the statistics and the QSO and heard station lists.
  • Message log: every frame heard, newest at the bottom. The Type column labels each row (TO ME, CQ, MY TX, MY DIGI, TLM, BEACON) and the row colour matches the legend, so colour is never the only cue. Hover a row to see the raw frame. Double-click a row to put that station in the To box.
  • QSO: the stations you are working, with their state, UHM status and whether they are logged.
  • Heard stations: everyone heard this session, with the time since last heard, CQ and frame counts, the satellite's elevation from their grid, their distance and bearing from you, and whether they are a new call, a new grid or already worked. Click a station to put it in the To box.

Portable and mobile: Terminal uses your station location for your grid, the grid placeholders in macros, the grid saved with logged QSOs, and the elevation and LOS figures. To follow a GPS, set it up in Settings → GPS and turn on Automatically update station position from GPS fix. When GPS is set up, a GPS or Saved label beside your grid shows which location is in use; hover over it for details. Without a recent fix, Terminal keeps the last known location. When you cross into a new four-character grid square, the status line says so, so you can check any macro with your grid typed in.

The message log, QSO and Heard stations boxes each have A− and A+ buttons to change the text size. Hold Ctrl and turn the mouse wheel over a box for the same effect. The columns grow with the text, and each box remembers its own size. Each box also has a Clear button. On the message log it removes the decoded frames. On QSO it takes those stations out of the QSO list and leaves them under Heard stations. On Heard stations it removes every station, which also empties the QSO list. Statistics stay as they are; use Reset beside the statistics to zero those counts.

Drag the thin lines between the column headings to change a column's width. Double-click one to put that column back to its default width. You can also drag the gap between the message log and the QSO and Heard stations boxes, and the gap between those two boxes. OscarWatch remembers text sizes, column widths and these divider positions next time you open the Terminal.

Macros

Edit macros in Settings → Macros. These placeholders are filled in when you send:

  • {CALL}: the call in the To box
  • {MYCALL}: your callsign
  • {MYGRID}: your full grid; {MYGRID4}: its first four characters

Tick To CQ for a macro that should always go to CQ (the default F1). The defaults are CQ {MYGRID4}, {MYGRID4} 599, R73 and 73 TU. A macro whose text is just {POSITION} sends your position instead of a message (see below).

APRS positions and the Terminal Map

Many stations on the ISS and IO-117 send APRS position reports. Terminal decodes uncompressed positions (with or without a timestamp), compressed positions and Mic-E. The station's grid and its distance and bearing from you fill in under Heard stations. A grid the station types in a message always takes priority over one worked out from its position. For Mic-E, the radio's type characters are removed from the comment, the altitude is decoded, and a status such as En route or Emergency is shown at the start of the message. Anything that does not decode is shown as plain text, as before.

Press the globe button (Terminal Map) at the top of the Terminal to open a map of the heard stations that have a known location, with your station and the focused satellite's footprint, so you can see who is in range now. A station with an APRS position is drawn at that position, and one with only a grid is drawn at the grid square centre. Each station is a square with its callsign beside it; hover over it for the grid, distance and time last heard, or click it to put that station in the To box.

  • This pass only: shows only stations heard since the current pass began, or since the start of the most recent pass while the satellite is below the horizon.
  • Fade after: stations not heard for this many minutes are drawn faded with a dashed outline, and are removed after twice that time.
  • Zoom: when an area such as Europe gets crowded, zoom in with the mouse wheel or the + and − buttons, and drag the map to move around. With the map focused, the + and − keys zoom, Shift and the arrow keys move the map, and 0 goes back to the whole world, as does Whole world. Markers and callsigns stay the same size as you zoom, so close stations separate.
  • Clearing the Heard stations list also clears the map.

Sending your position

Send Position, next to Send, transmits an APRS position report through the satellite, so your station shows on maps such as aprs.fi when an iGate hears it. It only works with an APRS satellite profile. Hover over the button to see the location it will send. OscarWatch takes your location from, in order:

  1. a GPS fix less than two minutes old, when a GPS is set up in Settings;
  2. the station latitude and longitude in Settings;
  3. the centre of your station grid square.

In Terminal settings → Position you can choose the precision, the APRS symbol and the comment. About 2 km (the default) and About 20 km blank digits of the position, as APRS allows, and Grid square centre sends the middle of your four-character grid square, so your exact location is not shared. The comment can use {MYGRID4}, {MYGRID} and {MYCALL}. To keep the satellite clear for everyone, you can send a position at most once a minute, and Terminal never beacons on its own.

Logging

With Log contacts automatically on (the default), a contact is saved once you and the other station have both sent a grid or report, and a roger or 73 has gone either way. It works whether you answered their CQ or they answered yours. QSOs are saved to the current logbook with mode PKT, propagation mode SAT, the satellite name and frequencies, and are uploaded to Cloudlog and sent over the satellite link when those are set up. Press F12 to log the station in the To box at any time.

You Heard Me (UHM)

UHM on OscarWatch.org tells you when a station you heard has also reported hearing you, so you know the path works both ways even if their reply was lost. To use it, save your OscarWatch.org API token in Settings → OscarWatch, then tick Enable UHM in Terminal settings → UHM. Test connection checks the token and warns you if the account callsign is not the one Terminal transmits.

  • Terminal reports stations whose frames are addressed to you. CQ calls, beacons, telemetry and your own echoes are not reported, and the same station is reported at most once every 30 seconds.
  • For your QSO partners and the call in the To box, Terminal asks UHM every 7 seconds whether they heard you. The UHM column then shows Wait, Heard me, Mutual or Expired. User means the station uses UHM on this satellite.
  • Match on message text also sends the decoded text, so UHM confirms the exact frame.

The UHM state and any message from the server appear in the status bar.

APRS-IS gateway

In Settings → APRS-IS you can forward the packets you hear to APRS-IS as a receive-only iGate, so they show on sites such as aprs.fi. Enter your APRS-IS passcode; without a valid one the server accepts the login but drops the packets. Position reports, including Mic-E and compressed positions, are forwarded just like messages, so stations you hear through the satellite appear on the map at aprs.fi. Packets marked NOGATE, RFONLY or TCPIP are never forwarded, and duplicates within 30 seconds are dropped. Terminal never transmits packets from the internet.

Problems?

  • "Modem: cannot reach 127.0.0.1:8000": the soundmodem is not running, or its AGW server is off or on another port.
  • Connected but nothing appears: check the soundmodem decodes packets on its own, that the right Radio port is chosen, and that the radio is tuned (Doppler) to the downlink.
  • IO-117 shows nothing: Direwolf has no BPSK modem; use the UZ7HO soundmodem.
  • Your frames never come back: check PTT and transmit audio level in the soundmodem, and the uplink frequency. The statistics panel shows how many of your frames were digipeated.
  • UHM says the token was not accepted: create a new token on OscarWatch.org and save it in Settings.
